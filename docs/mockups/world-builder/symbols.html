The rules

  1. Straight top-down, like the land under it. A roof is two halves, lit and shadowed, with the light from the north-west, the same light the hills are shaded by. That is what makes a rectangle read as a building.
  2. One ink, one outline weight. Every outline is #1b2124 at the same width, so everything looks drawn by one hand.
  3. Only people carry their people's colour. Buildings are their material: timber, stone or thatch. Vehicles and animals are neutral. A person in a boat, on a horse or at a cart shows whose it is. Factions are the only loud colour on the map.
  4. One detail says the job: the mill's sails, the smithy's glowing chimney, the temple's columns, the battlements of a fort or a tower, the market's striped stalls, the water in a well.
  5. The land has few marks, always the same few. A tree is a crown and a highlight; a field is furrows; grass is its colour and a tuft or two.
  6. People are circles. A child is a smaller one. A leader has a white ring, a soldier a shield and a spear. What is selected or followed is a ring round the circle, as it is now.
