The symbol set

Everything the People map draws close in, straight from above. Second draft, for Ric to judge; nothing here is on the real page yet.

The rules

  1. Straight top-down, like the land under it.
  2. Light and shade, never an outline. The sun is in the north-west, as for the hills. A roof is three or four facets, lit, mid and dark. Everything that stands up casts a soft shadow to the south-east, which is what gives it height.
  3. Footprints vary from a few parts: a main block, a wing, a lean-to, a chimney. On the map the parts are picked from the seed and the building's own number, so no two houses in a village are alike and the same house always looks the same.
  4. A tight palette. Roofs are terracotta, slate, thatch or pale stone. Wood is one brown, metal one grey-green. Only people carry their people's colour, with a highlight so they read as round. Bandits, who belong to no people, are a dull brown.
  5. One detail says the job: the mill's sails, the smithy's glow, the temple's columns, battlements, stalls, water, a pit, a pennant.
  6. The world's state is a light over everything, not a new set of colours: night is a blue shade with lit windows; winter whitens what faces up; fire is the only bright warm colour, and its smoke drifts with the wind.
  7. People are circles. A child is a smaller one, a leader has a white ring, what is selected a solid ring, what is followed a dashed one.
  8. Real size, with a floor. A person is drawn half a metre across, as the engine has them, but never smaller than about five pixels on screen; close in they grow with the land, so a house really is some thirty people long. The tiles below are not to scale; the scenes are.
  9. Three zooms, three drawings. Close in, everything here. In the middle, a house is two roof tones and a hard shadow, a person a dot, a tree one crown, with no chimneys, highlights or blur. Far out, villages and land only; people are not drawn one by one.
  10. Roofs stay dull. Weathered clay, straw and slate, well away from every people's colour; fire is the only bright warm thing on the map. Smoke drifts with the wind and fades.
