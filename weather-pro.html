Official watches and warnings are the National Weather Service's, and the outlook is the Storm Prediction Center's. This page shows them; it does not issue them. In a warning, act on the warning.

Forecast numbers and model soundings: Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0). Observations, office forecasts and alerts: NWS. Radar and weather balloons: NOAA, via Iowa Environmental Mesonet.

Where?

    Saved

      A place you choose is kept in this browser only. It is rounded to about a kilometre before anything is asked about it, and it is never stored by this site. Without one, the page shows the whole country.

      Watches

      Conditions at your saved place this page keeps an eye on — checked every two minutes while this page is open, and kept in this browser only. A reminder, not a warning service: the NWS's warnings are shown as it issues them, whatever you set here.

        Colour tables

        Your own colours for any radar product or model field. Values are in the unit the key shows; colours blend between them. Read a GRLevelX or GR2Analyst .pal colour table, or save yours as one. Kept in this browser only.

          3D radar

          Each point is a gate of one tilt, where the beam was: up the beam as the standard atmosphere bends it, and along the ground under it. Gaps between tilts are real — the radar does not look there. Drag to turn, wheel or pinch to zoom; arrow keys and + / − work too.

          Satellite recipe

          An RGB of your own from GOES's sixteen bands. Each colour is one band, or one band minus another, stretched from low (none of that colour) to high (all of it) — reflectance in per cent, temperatures in °C; low above high turns it round. A gamma above 1 lifts the dark end. Kept in this browser; a shared link carries the recipe with it.

          How this page is checked

          Weather Pro reads the agencies' own files and does its own arithmetic on them in your browser. This is what that arithmetic is held to, how its forecasts are graded, and what it cannot do. It is a hobby project, tested but not certified — never anyone's only source when it matters.

          Held to the libraries meteorologists use

          • Radar files (Level III) — against MetPy, on real files.
          • Model files (GRIB2, every packing read here) — against ecCodes, ECMWF's own library.
          • Satellite and lightning files (GOES, HDF5) — against h5py; the satellite's map projection against pyproj. The older HDF5 that ERA5's files before about 2023 use — against a file h5py wrote.
          • Satellite recipes — each standard RGB written as a recipe gives that RGB's own colours, to a billionth.
          • Full-volume radar (Level II) — its compression against Python's bzip2, its values against the files' own.
          • Soundings — CAPE, CIN, the parcel levels, helicity, shear and the composites against MetPy.

          Graded against what happened

          • Forecasts — every night the models' temperatures are scored against airport stations; the page uses its own blend only where a station's record says it beats the standard forecast. The record card shows that record for the station nearest your place.
          • The rain nowcast — graded every night with the same code and settings the page uses.

          Tested on cases with known answers only

          No independent library to compare with, so each is held to made-up cases whose answer is known, and to real files for sense:

          • Clutter filtering, the radar cross-section, velocity dealiasing, the VAD wind profile, Kuchera snowfall, difference maps, terrain, route weather, watches, where each ERA5 hour is read from, GR colour tables read and written back, and where the 3D radar puts each gate.

          What it cannot do

          • Watches are checked only while this page is open — there is no server sending them. Official warnings always come from the NWS.
          • Route weather assumes a steady speed and reads each point at the model hour nearest your arrival: stops, traffic and terrain change that.
          • Dealiasing is a simple continuity method and can choose wrong in noisy data; the VWP is one volume, where the NWS's shows the last hour.
          • The storm probabilities are a time-lagged HRRR, not HREF; the mesoanalysis is worked out SPC's way but is not SPC's; lightning is GOES flashes, not ground strikes.
          • The UKMET is drawn at 20 km; GFS-Wave is not read yet (its files need a JPEG 2000 decoder); tides, currents, buoys, river gauges and aviation reports are not here yet.
          • ERA5 runs two to three months behind (precipitation a month more), and its upper air is drawn every 6 hours: each of those maps is about 40 MB to fetch.
          • The 3D radar is one volume, thinned past 400,000 points, with its height stretched — the view says by how much.
          • Feed ages are shown live in "Where this comes from", but a history of outages is not kept.
