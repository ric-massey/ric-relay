Starfield · a faster-than-light explorer

You are flying a real relativistic rocket — then cheating past lightspeed on purpose.

The ship has real momentum and a six-gear drive, and the first two gears are honest relativity — gear 2 takes you to 99% of light, where your clock slows and the road ahead contracts. Only gears 3 and up break the speed limit, they say so while they do it, and every readout tells you exactly what the cheat costs.

▸ Launch flight

The ship has momentum

Nothing here is on rails. You carry a real velocity, and thrust adds to it: W pushes your momentum the way the nose is pointing, S pushes it the other way, and letting go of both leaves you coasting forever. The arrow keys turn the nose and nothing else — so you can look wherever you like at full speed while still travelling the way you were. The green marker is where you are actually going; the crosshair is where you are aimed. When they come apart, that is momentum, and closing the gap costs thrust. B is a full stop, and it works from any speed at all.

A gear is an acceleration, not a speed

Changing gear never changes how fast you are going. It changes how hard the engine can push, and how fast it is allowed to push you to. Drop into a low gear at speed and you keep every bit of that speed — the engine simply stops being able to add more. That is what makes gear 1 usable for creeping up on a planet and gear 6 usable for crossing to another galaxy.

Gears 1 and 2 are real physics. Gear 2 climbs to 99% of light, and that is where relativity earns its keep: at that speed your clock runs seven times slower than home and the road ahead is contracted by the same seven, so you cross a solar system in about a minute without ever breaking the speed limit. Gears 3–6 are the declared cheat, and the game says so on screen while they are running.

What the panel is telling you

  • Ship time vs Home time. Two clocks. While you are genuinely below lightspeed they come apart — your clock runs slow, everyone back home ages faster. That gap is the honest heart of the game.
  • Felt g-force. The real acceleration a pilot would feel right now — honest even when the drive is cheating. In the high gears it reads openly in the millions of g. Nothing is hidden; the cost is just shown.
  • Speed. A percentage of light in the honest gears — you can never reach c, however hard you push — and light-years per second once the drive cheats. At high γ you cover far more ground per second of your life than the percentage suggests, because the distance ahead of you is genuinely contracted.
  • Closing on. How fast you are travelling toward the nearest world. Zero when you are parked, which is the number that tells you whether you are about to hit it.
  • Hull. Fly too close to a star and its heat burns you. Watch the bar.

Curious where the physics stops and the cheats begin? The ship carries an honesty ledger — the “where we cheat” button — listing every fudge with the arithmetic. The relativity underneath is written up in The Geometry of Spacetime.
