Starfield · a faster-than-light explorer

You are flying a real relativistic rocket — then cheating past lightspeed on purpose.

Space here is drawn at light-year scale: even the nearest planet in a system is a couple of light-years off, so at honest lightspeed it is a two-year trip. That is why your drive is faster-than-light by design. The physics it is built on stays honest — the drive cheats, and every readout tells you exactly how much.

▸ Launch flight

The drive works like a car

Hold W and you accelerate toward the current gear's top speed. Let go and you keep that speed — the drive coasts, so you set a speed once and fly. S slows you back down, and if you keep holding it past a standstill you reverse and back away from whatever you were flying at. B is a full stop from either direction. Turning the ship steers where you go — point the nose and the whole sky wheels to follow, in reverse too.

Six gears

Each gear is a speed range, and that is the point of having six: acceleration is a fixed share of the gear's ceiling, so the gear you pick is the precision you get. Gear 2 parks you next to a planet; gear 6 gets you to Andromeda. Shift down and the drive brakes you to the new gear's ceiling. Only gear 1 stays inside the speed of light.

What the panel is telling you

  • Ship time vs Home time. Two clocks. While you are genuinely below lightspeed they come apart — your clock runs slow, everyone back home ages faster. That gap is the honest heart of the game.
  • Felt g-force. The real acceleration a pilot would feel right now — honest even when the drive is cheating. In the high gears it reads openly in the millions of g. Nothing is hidden; the cost is just shown.
  • Speed. A percentage of light while sub-light; light-years per real second once the drive goes faster-than-light.
  • Hull. Fly too close to a star and its heat burns you. Watch the bar.

Curious where the physics stops and the cheats begin? The ship carries an honesty ledger — the “where we cheat” button — listing every fudge with the arithmetic. The relativity underneath is written up in The Geometry of Spacetime.
