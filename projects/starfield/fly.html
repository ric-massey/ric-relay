← how to fly Starfield is an interactive flight through the real solar neighbourhood, drawn on a canvas. You fly a rocket whose throttle is measured in gravities, and the game computes the actual consequences: aberration crushes the stars into a cone ahead of you, the Doppler effect paints them into a coloured ring, the cosmic microwave background heats up until it is as bright as the Sun, and the clock aboard your ship falls behind the clock at home. Playing it needs a canvas-capable browser, but the physics it is built on is written up in The Geometry of Spacetime.

Honesty ledger

WHERE WE CHEAT

Everything else in this game is real physics. These are the places it is not, with the arithmetic — because the honest version of this game is unlosable. Flying in a straight line through the real galaxy you would cover about 24 quadrillion light-years before hitting a star, roughly a million times the width of the observable universe.

Controls

FLIGHT CONTROLS

Click any key to rebind it, then press the key you want. Rotating the ship and moving it are separate — pointing the nose somewhere is not the same as going there, and the gap between the crosshair and the green heading dot is the whole flight model.

Star chart

WHERE TO?

Pick a destination and an amber marker guides you to it. Real catalogued stars, galaxies and black holes, nearest first. Fly sub-light to a nearby star to park and look; jump (J) for the far ones.
