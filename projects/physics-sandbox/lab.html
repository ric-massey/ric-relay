Tools

Place: tap or drag to put the chosen kind down (tap one to change its kind). Bond: tap one hexagon, then a touching one, to bond them — or unbond them if they already are. Pin: tap to hold one still. Erase: tap or drag.

A soup to work with

Put down on random empty cells, unbonded — the raw material a template could copy from.

Start again

Kinds

What each kind is. Bonds: how many it can hold. Pull: how hard it pulls on what a bond shares; with charge on, the harder puller goes negative. Shape: where its bonds may sit — straight, bent at 120°, a Y, a cross, or anywhere; it only counts when “Bonds hold an angle” is on in Rules.

Who bonds with whom

How strong a bond between each pair is, in energy. 0 means they never bond. Both halves of the table are kept the same.

The same physics as the sandbox. What the world is made of comes from the grid and the kinds, so those settings are not here.

Run an experiment

Runs the setup as built and a control side by side on the grid, where you can watch them: the control is the same hexagons scattered loose with no bonds and nothing pinned, for the same time on the same world seeds. Whatever the built world ends with more of than the control — beyond what it started with — is the only thing building it can take credit for. That is how copying is told apart from things simply assembling.
